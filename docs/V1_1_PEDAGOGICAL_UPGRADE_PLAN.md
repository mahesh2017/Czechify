# Czechify Version 1.1 — Pedagogical Upgrade Plan

**Scope:** A1 Units 1–10 only
**Prepared:** 2026-08-01
**Status:** Research complete; Unit 1 implemented and technically validated; Units 2–10 remain planned

**Unit 1 implementation (2026-08-01):** Four lessons, 44 activities, eight
picture-led words, seven listening-comprehension activities, eight pronunciation
activities, and a final listening/speaking/writing repair mission. Both Czech audio
voices have complete local and deployed coverage. Automated validation passes; an
independent Czech-language/pedagogical review and beginner device pilot remain
required before calling the content field-validated.

## 1. Executive recommendation

Version 1.1 should preserve the current grammar dependency order, but change the
learner's experience from **grammar topic → drills** to **real-life mission → useful
language → focused grammar → guided use → delayed transfer**.

The first ten units should become a coherent beginner journey in which the learner
can:

1. decode and repair a basic Czech exchange;
2. greet and introduce themselves appropriately;
3. identify common people and things;
4. say who they are and what they have;
5. talk about common activities;
6. request and order basic items;
7. identify owners and possessions;
8. introduce and describe family;
9. exchange numbers and arrange a time;
10. describe a simple daily routine.

Grammar remains important because Czech is highly inflected, but it is taught as a
tool for accomplishing these missions. The Czech A1 reference description makes
this distinction explicitly: A1 success is not knowing the rule for the feminine
accusative; it is being able to request water, coffee, advice, or help in the
appropriate situation.

The current content is useful raw material, not a bad foundation. It already has
audio-capable teaching rows, pronunciation, dictation, answer feedback, SRS,
reading/listening/speaking/writing widgets, and two Czech audio voices. The main
pedagogical problems are composition and transfer:

- Units are named and sequenced primarily as grammar topics.
- Most teaching is one large card followed by selected or tightly constrained
  response exercises.
- Dialogues are text-completion exercises, and their lines currently have no audio.
- Units 2–10 have no lesson-specific images.
- Learners are not required to understand a new short recording, read a functional
  text, formulate a personal response, or complete an end-of-unit real-world task.
- The full alphabet, number system, conjugation tables, or pronoun inventory is
  often presented in one pass, creating unnecessary beginner load.

## 3. Research foundation

### 3.1 Begin with communicative outcomes

The CEFR treats learners as social agents and recommends working backwards from
real-world needs and “can do” outcomes instead of building a syllabus as a linear
sequence of structures. At A1, the learner should be able to interact simply about
personal details and concrete needs when the other person speaks slowly and helps.

The official Czech A1 reference description is even more directly relevant. It
recommends:

- selecting language from likely situations and roles;
- using grammar immediately in real communication;
- learning new vocabulary together with grammatical information, sound, and
  spelling;
- training learners to extract key information from recordings without needing to
  understand every word;
- teaching compensation and repair phrases from the beginning;
- treating grammar as instrumental rather than as the final learning objective.

### 3.2 Balance four kinds of learning

Paul Nation's four-strands framework recommends a broadly balanced course with:

1. meaning-focused input;
2. meaning-focused output;
3. language-focused learning;
4. fluency development using language the learner already knows.

The balance should be measured by meaningful learning time, not by mechanically
making exactly 25% of exercise records each type.

### 3.3 Retrieve, correct, and return later

Spacing has a medium-to-large effect in second-language learning, with longer
spacing especially helpful for delayed retention. Applied vocabulary research also
finds that retrieval works best when it is paired with corrective feedback. Therefore
each important word, chunk, and pattern should be recalled before reveal, corrected,
retried with a variation, and encountered again in later units.

### 3.4 Use images and audio purposefully

Recent meta-analysis supports combining an L1 meaning and a relevant picture for
initial L2 vocabulary learning. Multimedia-learning research also supports relevant
words plus visuals, spatially close labels, removal of decorative material, and
short learner-controlled segments.

This does **not** justify filling every screen with simultaneous narration, captions,
animation, and decoration. Czechify should use:

- concrete images for concrete meanings;
- diagrams for relationships or transformations;
- short storyboards for interactions and sequence;
- learner-controlled audio with replay and slow options;
- transcripts after the first listening attempt;
- no image where an image adds no semantic value.

### 3.5 Train perception as well as pronunciation

High-variability phonetic training—varied phonetic contexts, more than one speaker,
and immediate feedback—has a medium-to-large effect on L2 speech perception and
generalises substantially to untrained items and speakers. Unit 1 should therefore
include sound identification and contrast work before asking for production. The
goal is intelligibility, not elimination of accent or automatic perfection on **ř**.

## 4. Version 1.1 learning architecture

Each unit should contain four short core lessons plus a delayed transfer task.

### Lesson A — Scene and meaning

- State one learner-facing “I can…” outcome.
- Show one relevant scene or short storyboard.
- Play a short Czech exchange before presenting a grammar explanation.
- Ask one gist question, then one concrete-detail question.
- Reveal 8–12 core words/chunks through image, audio, and concise English meaning.

### Lesson B — Pattern and sound

- Extract one productive language pattern from the scene.
- Add at most one secondary recognition-only pattern.
- Use a visual transformation, color cue, timeline, or contrast only when it explains
  meaning.
- Include a perception task before pronunciation production.
- Keep complete paradigms available as reference, but do not require memorising an
  entire table before useful language can be produced.

### Lesson C — Guided use

- Retrieve the new language before showing it again.
- Include at least one short listening and one short functional reading.
- Move from recognition to cued production.
- Recycle language from at least two earlier units.
- Give focused feedback, immediate retry, and a structurally similar variation.

### Lesson D — Mission

- Give the learner a real outcome: introduce themselves, obtain an item, identify an
  owner, arrange a meeting, or describe a day.
- Combine at least two skills rather than presenting another list of isolated items.
- Offer help in a ladder: replay → focused cue → word bank → model.
- Record first-attempt success separately from success after support.
- End with a short personal spoken or written product the learner can recognise as
  useful outside the app.

### Delayed transfer

The next unit and the SRS/review path should bring back the previous mission in a new
voice, image, or context. Immediate lesson completion is not mastery. The useful
evidence is successful performance after a delay with less support.

## 5. Content guardrails

These are authoring constraints for Units 1–10:

- **Active vocabulary:** normally 10–14 new lexical items per unit.
- **Useful chunks:** 4–7 new multiword expressions per unit.
- **Grammar:** one productive pattern and at most one secondary recognition target
  per unit lesson; full tables live in reference material.
- **Recycling:** every unit must reuse at least six items/chunks from earlier units
  and explicitly list what it recycles.
- **Encounters:** every core item receives at least eight meaningful encounters:
  input, in-unit retrieval, contextual use, and later spaced retrieval.
- **Exercise balance:** multiple choice and translation together should be no more
  than 25% of the learning time in a unit.
- **Skills:** every unit includes listening, perception/pronunciation, reading, and
  spoken output. From Unit 2 onward, every unit also includes a tiny functional
  writing task.
- **Czech first:** English supports comprehension but is normally revealed after a
  prediction or first attempt.
- **Register:** teach formal/informal choices in context, not as an isolated note.
- **Errors:** correct errors that block meaning or the unit target; do not punish
  harmless A1 variation as if the learner were writing formal C2 Czech.
- **New forms:** an unanalysed form may appear as a clearly taught survival chunk,
  but a graded task may not silently depend on unexplained grammar.
- **Pronunciation:** success means understandable Czech. Automated transcript or
  phoneme feedback must not claim to measure accent or CEFR attainment.

## 6. Unit-by-unit blueprint

### Unit 1 — Hear, Read, and Repair Czech

**Mission:** Understand and reproduce familiar Czech words, spell a name, and ask
for slower speech or repetition.

**Can do:**

- I can recognise first-syllable stress and meaningful vowel length in familiar
  words.
- I can connect the most important Czech spellings to sounds.
- I can say **Prosím pomalu**, **Ještě jednou, prosím**, **Nerozumím**, and
  **Jak se to píše?**

**Language focus:** Czech rhythm; short/long vowels; c/č, s/š, z/ž, h/ch, r/ř,
and ě in useful words. Letter names are retained as an optional spelling/reference
tool, not the main gate to Unit 2.

**Media:** Reuse the eight existing picture words; add a compact mouth-position
diagram for the few sounds where it helps, visual stress/length marking, and two-
voice identification sets in varied words. No promise that the learner will
“master ř” in one lesson.

**Mission evidence:** Identify ten heard words without text, read five familiar
words, record two repair phrases, and spell a personal name.

### Unit 2 — Meet Someone

**Mission:** Greet a stranger or a friend and exchange basic personal information.

**Can do:**

- I can choose a formal or informal greeting.
- I can give my name and origin and ask another person's name.
- I can close the exchange politely and repair it if speech is too fast.

**Language focus:** **Dobrý den/Ahoj**, **Jak se jmenujete/jmenuješ?**,
**Jmenuju se…**, **Jsem z…**, **Těší mě**, **Na shledanou/Ahoj**; productive
**jsem/jste** as chunks; question intonation; **ty/vy** in context.

**Media:** Two contrasting scenes—reception/office and friendly social meeting;
three micro-dialogues with varied names, origins, and two Czech voices; a simple
formal-versus-informal visual.

**Mission evidence:** Complete and record a 20–30 second introduction, then respond
appropriately to one formal and one informal greeting from a new voice.

**Recycles:** repair phrases and sound patterns from Unit 1.

### Unit 3 — People and Things Around Me

**Mission:** Ask and say who or what something is.

**Can do:**

- I can ask **Kdo je to?** and **Co je to?**
- I can identify familiar people and objects with **To je…**
- I can recognise the usual masculine, feminine, and neuter noun cues.

**Language focus:** noun gender learned with each noun; **to je**; **kdo/co**;
productive **ten/ta/to** only with a small set of concrete nouns. Gender is a
meaningful retrieval feature, not only a colored label.

**Media:** Twelve reusable object/person cards balanced across genders; one room or
street scene for “find and identify”; a three-column gender diagram showing endings
and exceptions without presenting gender as biological.

**Mission evidence:** Identify six items in a new scene and ask/answer three
**kdo/co** questions.

**Recycles:** greetings, names, **to**, pronunciation of diacritics.

### Unit 4 — Who I Am and What I Have

**Mission:** Exchange simple facts about identity, status, and possessions.

**Can do:**

- I can say and ask whether someone is a student, worker, or at home.
- I can say what common things I have or do not have.
- I can form a basic yes/no question and negation with **být** and **mít**.

**Language focus:** high-frequency forms of **být/mít** in useful sentences;
pronoun omission; **nejsem/nemám**; **Jsi/Jste…? Máš/Máte…?** Complete six-form
tables remain accessible as reference after the scene.

**Media:** Profile cards, a bag/desk inventory, two short “new colleague/new
neighbour” exchanges, and a compact positive/question/negative pattern diagram.

**Mission evidence:** Complete a personal profile and conduct a four-question mini
interview using a mix of **být** and **mít**.

**Recycles:** Unit 2 introductions and Unit 3 noun gender.

### Unit 5 — What I Do

**Mission:** Ask and answer what someone does, where they live, and which language
they speak or understand.

**Can do:**

- I can say basic activities such as working, studying, living, speaking, reading,
  and understanding.
- I can ask **Co děláš/děláte?**, **Kde bydlíš/bydlíte?**, and
  **Rozumíš/rozumíte?**
- I can recognise person from a common present-tense verb ending.

**Language focus:** teach **já/ty/vy** forms first inside useful questions and
answers; introduce three present-tense patterns through model verbs; move full
paradigms to reference. Include **v Praze/v Brně** as useful fixed chunks rather
than opening a locative-case syllabus here.

**Media:** Four illustrated person profiles with job/activity/language cues; three
short interviews; a verb-ending signal diagram that highlights meaning before
terminology.

**Mission evidence:** Match new voices to profiles, then ask and answer four
personal activity questions.

**Recycles:** introductions, **být**, places, question/repair strategies.

### Unit 6 — Get What You Need

**Mission:** Request or order basic food, drink, and everyday items politely.

**Can do:**

- I can ask whether an item is available.
- I can order or request one or two items.
- I can understand a simple confirmation and repair a misunderstanding.

**Language focus:** **Dám si…**, **Chci…**, **Prosím…**, **Máte…?**; accusative
through immediately useful object phrases. Productive priority is feminine
**-a → -u** plus unchanged common neuter/masculine-inanimate forms. Less useful
animate patterns can remain recognition/reference at this point.

**Media:** A Czech café counter/menu scene, twelve concrete item cards, a concise
“what changes?” transformation visual, and three clear transactions. A later pass
may add mild real-world noise only after the clear version is understood.

**Mission evidence:** Listen for the requested item, select an appropriate response,
and complete a two-item spoken order without seeing the full model.

**Recycles:** formal greeting, **mít**, yes/no, gender, repair phrases.

### Unit 7 — My Things and Your Things

**Mission:** Identify belongings and return an item to its owner.

**Can do:**

- I can ask whether an item belongs to someone.
- I can say **my/your/his/her** with common belongings.
- I can use or omit personal pronouns naturally in short familiar sentences.

**Language focus:** productive **můj/moje**, **tvůj/tvoje**, **jeho/její** and
high-frequency personal pronouns; recognition of **náš/váš/jejich**. Avoid asking
the learner to memorise the entire possessive inventory in one card.

**Media:** Lost-property scene, owner-and-belonging matching cards, short exchanges
with contrasting owners, and a gender-agreement visual reusing Unit 3 colors plus
text labels.

**Mission evidence:** Hear three lost-property exchanges and correctly return four
items, then record two ownership questions and answers.

**Recycles:** noun gender, **mít**, questions, accusative object phrases.

### Unit 8 — My Family and People I Know

**Mission:** Introduce family or other important people and give a basic description.

**Can do:**

- I can name common family relationships.
- I can introduce someone with **To je můj/moje…**
- I can give one or two simple qualities with correct basic gender agreement.

**Language focus:** a practical family set; **Kdo je to? Jaký/á je?**; nominative
hard-adjective agreement through a small productive set such as **milý/milá**,
**mladý/mladá**, **velký/velká**, and **malý/malá**.

**Media:** One reusable family tree, six diverse portrait cards, a short voice note
describing a photo, and a visual agreement pattern. Imagery must represent varied
families without making relationship, nationality, or profession stereotypes.

**Mission evidence:** Build a three-person family/people card and give a short spoken
or written introduction using at least two descriptions.

**Recycles:** possessives, gender, **být**, names, age as preview only if supported.

### Unit 9 — Numbers and Making a Plan

**Mission:** Exchange contact details and arrange a simple meeting.

**Can do:**

- I can understand and say common numbers, including a phone number.
- I can ask and tell the time.
- I can understand a day/date and agree on a basic meeting time.

**Language focus:** numbers 0–20, tens, and composition to 100; phone-number
grouping; **Kolik je hodin?**, **V kolik?**, **dnes/zítra**, days of the week;
24-hour time in schedules. Months and full calendar dates are taught in a separate
short segment so they do not compete with number formation and clock time at once.

**Media:** Learner-controlled number audio, analogue/digital clock cards, a weekly
calendar, a contact card, and several very short recordings containing numbers,
times, and dates from both voices.

**Mission evidence:** Transcribe a phone number and appointment detail from a new
voice, then propose and confirm a meeting time.

**Recycles:** personal details, **mít čas**, present-tense questions, repair and
confirmation phrases.

### Unit 10 — My Day

**Mission:** Understand and describe a simple daily routine in chronological order.

**Can do:**

- I can say when I get up, work/study, eat, return home, relax, and sleep.
- I can use a small set of **se/si** verbs as complete lexical chunks.
- I can ask another person about their routine and identify key differences.

**Language focus:** **jmenovat se**, **mýt se/sprchovat se**, **oblékat se**,
**dívat se**, **čistit si zuby**, plus non-reflexive routine verbs; simple placement
in model sentences such as **Ráno se sprchuju**; time adverbs and sequence markers
**ráno, potom, odpoledne, večer**.

**Media:** A six-panel day timeline, routine action cards, two contrasting audio
diaries, and a movable word-order diagram for **se/si**. Use images to carry
sequence and meaning rather than adding decorative lifestyle scenes.

**Mission evidence:** Reconstruct a heard routine, compare it with another person's
day, and record or write a five-sentence personal routine without a full model.

**Recycles:** present verbs, time, family/people, possessives, **být/mít**, and
question forms from all earlier units.

## 7. Media production plan

### 7.1 Images

Plan the artwork as 30 designed compositions rather than commissioning unrelated
single images one by one:

- 10 unit scene/storyboard compositions;
- 10 vocabulary sheets containing 6–8 semantically clear tiles, exported as
  individual cards where interaction requires it;
- 10 diagrams for sound, grammar, ownership, time, or sequence.

The eight existing Unit 1 cards count toward this scope. Every image must have a
semantic label, a declared teaching purpose, visual consistency with Czechify, and
at least two planned uses. Concrete vocabulary can use a single clear object;
abstract grammar should use diagrams rather than metaphorical stock art.

### 7.2 Audio

- Every Czech model word, sentence, dialogue line, listening transcript, and mission
  prompt that the learner is expected to understand should have packaged/deployed
  neural audio.
- Use the existing female and male Czech voices; alternate speakers naturally in
  dialogues and include both across every unit.
- Provide normal and slow playback through controls; do not duplicate files solely
  to produce a slower speed unless quality testing shows rate-shifting is poor.
- Add “play line” and “play conversation” controls to dialogue exercises. At present
  dialogue lines are deliberately excluded from the audio-pack extractor because
  the dialogue widget cannot speak them.
- Listening progression is: gist without transcript → detail → replay/slow on demand
  → reveal transcript → shadow selected phrase → new-speaker transfer.
- Unit 1 perception sets should vary both voice and phonetic context and provide
  immediate corrective feedback.
- Native-speaker review must check stress, vowel length, connected speech, names,
  register, and whether the slowed version remains natural.

An initial planning estimate is 300–400 distinct new or substantially revised Czech
utterances for Units 1–10, resulting in roughly 600–800 voice files before duplicate
texts are collapsed by the existing hash-based audio pack.

## 8. Required product/content enablement

Most of the pedagogical work is content, but four small platform capabilities should
be completed before rewriting all ten units:

1. **Lesson outcome metadata:** add `can_do`, `new_language`, `recycles`, and
   `exit_task` metadata to the curriculum contract and display the can-do at lesson
   start/end.
2. **Audio-rich dialogues:** support per-line Czech audio, speaker voice assignment,
   and full-dialogue playback; include dialogue lines in audio extraction.
3. **Context images:** permit optional `image` and `image_label` fields in dialogue,
   listening, reading, and mission/speaking screens, not only teaching image cards.
4. **Content validation:** add checks for media existence, semantic labels, audio
   coverage, declared recycling, unsupported grammar, answer constructibility, and
   stable lesson order.

The existing listening, reading, speaking, and writing exercise views can be used in
Units 1–10 after content is authored; these are currently used only in later exam
content.

## 9. Implementation order

### Phase 1 — Build one representative vertical slice

Upgrade **Unit 2** first. It exercises scene art, formal/informal register, two-speaker
dialogue audio, listening, reading, speaking, writing, and a mission without the
special phonetic complexity of Unit 1.

Deliverables:

- the four lesson archetypes;
- lesson metadata and can-do UI;
- dialogue and context-image support;
- complete Unit 2 content, audio, images, tests, and native review.

Do not scale to nine more units until Unit 2 can be completed on both iOS and Android
without confusing instructions, excessive scrolling, missing audio, or unsupported
answers.

### Phase 2 — Rebuild Unit 1

Apply the validated shell to perception, spelling, intelligibility, and repair. Move
the complete alphabet recital to reference/optional practice and test transfer to a
new voice. **Implemented 2026-08-01:** the alphabet is now a compact non-graded
reference at the end of Lesson 3, and Lesson 4 finishes with a new-context repair
mission. Both hosted voices are available for all spoken Unit 1 material.

### Phase 3 — Build Units 3–5

Complete the identity and present-tense sequence. Run a cumulative mission after Unit
5: meet someone, identify people/things, and exchange personal/activity information.

### Phase 4 — Build Units 6–10

Complete request/ordering, ownership, family, scheduling, and routine. Add cumulative
missions after Units 8 and 10.

### Phase 5 — Independent review and beta validation

- one qualified Czech-as-a-foreign-language reviewer checks sequencing and task
  validity;
- one independent native Czech reviewer checks all language and audio;
- beginner testers from both Slavic and non-Slavic L1 backgrounds complete Units
  1–10;
- revise high-drop-off or high-support items before publishing the content release.

## 10. Acceptance criteria

### Per unit

- One clear real-world mission and learner-facing can-do statement.
- Four short lessons following Scene → Pattern → Guided use → Mission.
- 10–14 active lexical items and 4–7 useful chunks unless a documented reason says
  otherwise.
- At least two listening texts, one functional reading, one perception/pronunciation
  sequence, one spoken product, and one functional written product.
- At least two Czech voices and one new-speaker transfer item.
- At least one context scene/storyboard and one purposeful visual explanation.
- At least six explicitly recycled items from previous units.
- No missing image labels and no dialogue line without audio coverage.
- No lesson whose graded answer depends on an unexplained form.
- First-attempt result and supported/retried result remain distinguishable.

### Across Units 1–10

- All curriculum JSON passes contract, reference, answer, and media validation.
- All required Czech utterances have both-voice audio in the local and deployed
  manifest.
- Every exercise renders at phone widths, large text, light/dark mode, and with
  accessible labels.
- Unit missions are reviewed for real outcome, not merely renamed fill-in-the-blank
  drills.
- A delayed checkpoint tests Unit 1–5 and Unit 6–10 language in new contexts.
- Pronunciation claims use “understandable/intelligible” wording and do not claim
  accent diagnosis or CEFR certification.
- Any beta measurement is opt-in and privacy-consistent; production should not add
  silent analytics contrary to Czechify's current privacy promise.

## 11. Success measures for the pilot

Use these as product-learning targets, not pass/fail claims about an individual:

- at least 80% of pilot learners can complete the Unit 2 mission with no more than
  one focused cue after instruction;
- at least 70% can complete a varied introduction task 48–72 hours later;
- learners can identify the correct register in at least 4 of 5 novel contexts;
- listening success transfers to the second Czech voice rather than remaining tied
  to one speaker;
- transcript, slow-audio, and hint dependence decreases between guided use and the
  delayed task;
- native reviewers judge the target utterances intelligible and pragmatically
  appropriate.

These thresholds should be adjusted after the first small beta; they are product
diagnostics, not CEFR certification cut scores.
